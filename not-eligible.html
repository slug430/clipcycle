For creators who have been flagged

“Not eligible for the For You feed.”

If TikTok has told you a video is unoriginal, low quality or QR content, here is what that notice actually means, what TikTok has published about it, what creators report but TikTok has not confirmed, and what is worth doing next. Sources are linked so you can check every claim yourself.

The short version

Three things are true at once, and most advice online mixes them up.

01

It is not a strike

Being ineligible for the For You feed is not the same as a Community Guidelines removal. The video stays on your profile and your followers can still see it. It is held out of recommendations, which is why the views look like a cliff.

02

It is mostly about originality

TikTok’s originality policy treats content copied from others, reused without creative edits, or stitched from several sources with little added, as ineligible. Someone else’s watermark counts too.

03

It over-fires, and that part is unconfirmed

Creators widely report the flag landing on green-screen videos, text-heavy posts and faceless B-roll they made themselves. TikTok has not confirmed any of that, so treat it as a pattern rather than a rule.

What TikTok has actually published

Every line here comes from a TikTok page, linked. Anything we cannot point at, we have put in the next section instead.

  • Unoriginal means reused with nothing added. TikTok’s originality policy covers material copied from others, material mostly reused without creative edits, and material combined from several sources with little added value. TikTok Creator Academy
  • Another brand’s watermark counts against it. Content carrying someone else’s logo or watermark is treated as unoriginal — which catches clips exported from other editing apps. TikTok Creator Academy
  • Nothing reaches the feed until it clears moderation. Videos enter the recommendable pool only after passing content moderation. For You feed Eligibility Standards
  • Low views are not proof of a flag. TikTok says low views on an original video may simply reflect how the community responded. Check your own analytics before rewriting a hook that was fine. For You feed Eligibility Standards
  • Follower count is not a ranking factor. Neither is having had a video do well before. A flagged post does not put you in a hole you have to climb out of. For You feed Eligibility Standards
  • Originality affects money as well as reach. Unoriginal content is also ineligible for TikTok’s Creator Rewards Program, which uses originality as a key metric. TikTok Creator Academy

What creators report, which TikTok has not confirmed

Worth knowing because you will hear all of it anyway. Worth labelling because none of it can be checked.

That last one is worth singling out. The theory behind delete-and-repost is that a bad video drags down the next one — and that is the specific thing TikTok says does not happen, because past performance is not a direct ranking factor. Deleting also loses the comments, which are the most useful thing an underperforming post produces.

What to actually do

In the order worth doing it.

Check it really was flagged

Open the video’s analytics in TikTok. It will say if the post was made ineligible for recommendation. If it does not say so, the views are telling you something about the content rather than about a penalty.

Appeal it

If you made the video and it has been flagged, appeal in the app. It is free, it is the one remedy creators consistently say works, and nothing is lost by trying.

Look for a watermark

A logo in the corner from another editing app is the most common fixable cause, and the easiest to miss because you stop seeing it after a while.

Add something before reposting

If you are putting an old clip out again, it needs more than a new caption. A different hook, the old burned-in text covered, a tighter cut — a real edit, not a re-upload.

If you repost your own work, this rule is about you.

Reposting an old clip that still deserves an audience is a reasonable thing to do, and plenty of creators do it. But a straight re-upload is exactly what the originality policy describes, and “it is my own video” is not the test TikTok applies.

  • Change the opening — a new hook is the edit that matters most.
  • Cover text burned in last time, especially dates and old captions.
  • Re-cut it. The first couple of seconds are where attention is won or lost.
  • Strip any watermark from another app before it goes back out.

Common questions

Does being ineligible mean my account is shadowbanned?

“Shadowban” is not a TikTok term and it covers several different things people have experienced. What this particular notice means is narrower and documented: the video was held out of For You feed recommendations. Your profile, your followers and your other videos are not described as affected by it.

Will it clear on its own?

TikTok has not published a timescale, so anyone who gives you one is guessing. The appeal is the only remedy we can point to.

Is reposting my own old video against the rules?

Reposting is not banned. What the originality policy describes is reusing content without creative edits — so the question is not whether you own the clip, it is whether the thing you are posting is meaningfully different from the thing that went out before.

Does ClipCycle guarantee this will fix it?

No, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. Nobody outside TikTok knows the model, it changes, and plenty of good posts go nowhere for reasons no one can see.
